# Seasonal and trend confounders: target QA (September 2026)

Measurements behind `dot-SeasonalTrend-v1`
(`scripts/release_config_seasonal_trend.yaml`, `dotime.drivers`). No model was
trained, and the suite had not been built when this ran.

`qa.py` builds the first 100 episodes of each of the ten labels exactly as the
release build would. It takes the config's own per-episode specs (suite seed
20262001, T = 200, counterfactual pairing, 3 stability retries) and runs them
through `dotime._build.make_episode`. These are the first 100 episodes of each
label that the build will ship, 1,000 in total, generated by 4 worker processes.

## What is measured

- **Targets.** `y_obs` is the observational level of Y at the query (the onset
  step), `y_int` is `y_true`, and the effect is `y_int - y_obs`, the per-episode
  counterfactual effect under shared noise. Their nonzero fraction, mean and
  variance are asserted before anything else is computed. Both levels must be
  at least 50% nonzero with a finite mean and a positive variance, and some
  effects must be nonzero.
- **Driver variance share.** `var(x - x_twin) / var(x)` over the 200 released
  steps of the observational arm, for A and for Y. `x_twin` is the same episode
  rebuilt at driver strength 0, which draws exactly the same random numbers and
  reproduces the base structure bit for bit. So `x - x_twin` is what the driver
  adds, including its propagation through the dynamics. The script asserts that
  the rebuilt driven pair equals the released episode, column by column.
- **Effect-sign accuracy.** The contrast `pred(do A = v) - pred(do A = a_obs(t_q))`
  is compared with the true effect over the episodes with `|effect| >= 0.1`
  (column "Scored"), with a bootstrap 95% interval. A zero contrast names no
  direction and counts as wrong. The OLS columns use BackDoorOLS's own design,
  `Y_t ~ A_t + covariates_t + Y_(t-1)` fitted on the pre-onset rows, with three
  covariate sets. They are none, the base structure's back-door set (X for
  `back_door`, empty for `bi_variate`), which ignores D, and that set plus D. On
  observed labels the last one is exactly what BackDoorOLS fits, which the
  script asserts. On hidden labels D is rebuilt from `metadata["driver"]`, so
  that column is an oracle that no estimator of the released data has.
- **Level RMSE** of Mean, AR1, VAR-OLS and BackDoorOLS against `y_true`.

## Findings

1. Every label builds with no diverged episode after the 3 stability retries,
   and both level targets are nonzero in all 100 episodes of every label.
2. At the default strength of 1 the driver is a strong confounder. It accounts
   for a median 0.68 to 0.75 of the variance of A and Y on the seasonal labels,
   and 0.49 to 0.59 on the trend labels.
3. Adjusting for an observed driver helps. With BackDoorOLS's design,
   effect-sign accuracy on the seasonal labels is 0.60 to 0.67 without D and
   0.91 and 0.97 with it. On the trend labels it is 0.76 to 0.81 without D and
   0.90 and 0.94 with it. BackDoorOLS, which routes observed-driver labels to
   `{X, D}` or `{D}`, scores the same as the design with D.
4. A hidden seasonal driver reproduces the seasonal-confounding failure mode.
   Estimators that neither see D nor model time score 0.61 and 0.67 on the
   hidden seasonal labels, against 0.92 to 0.96 on the plain structures. The
   oracle that adjusts for the rebuilt D recovers 0.97 and 0.93, so the loss
   comes from the driver's confounding path, not from the base dynamics.
5. A hidden trend costs less, 0.91 and 0.79 against 0.96 and 0.92. One
   explanation, not tested here, is that the lagged outcome `Y_(t-1)` in the
   design stands in for a slowly moving confounder, which a seasonal driver
   with a period of 12 to 48 steps does not allow.
6. BackDoorOLS has the lowest level RMSE on `back_door`, both observed trend
   labels and `back_door+seasonal_observed`. On `bi_variate+seasonal_observed`
   AR1 is lower (0.592 against 0.803). BackDoorOLS predicts the interventional
   level averaged over the pre-onset rows of D instead of reading D at the
   query, which the known-future contract of an observed driver would allow.

## Caveats

- BackDoorOLS declines plain `bi_variate` (its routing of plain labels is
  unchanged) and every hidden-driver label. It then predicts the pre-onset
  mean, its contrast is 0, and it scores 0 by construction.
- NaiveOLS, a separate change, was not registered when this ran. `qa.py`
  scores it automatically once it is.
- With 100 episodes per label, 57 to 71 are scored, and the intervals are
  0.08 to 0.26 wide.
- Trend episodes are not stationary. They fall outside the
  stationarity-after-burn-in assumption of the paper's convergence result.

## Reproduce

Run from the repository root:

```
PYTHONPATH=src python results/reference/seasonal_trend/qa.py --workers 4
```

It takes about a minute on 4 cores and writes `seasonal_trend_qa.json`. The
output is deterministic, and a second run reproduced every number. The JSON
records the config's sha256, the suite seed and the commit the numbers were
produced at (`git_commit`, with `src_and_scripts_clean`). Cherry-picking
changes commit ids, so on another commit rerun the script and compare: every
entry under `labels` should come out identical while the generator code is.

These numbers are lower than the effect-contrast columns above because the protocols
differ. The packaged protocol scores `sign(pred - y_obs)`, where `pred` is the predicted
interventional level, so an estimator that averages the driver over the pre-onset rows
instead of reading it at the query (BackDoorOLS, finding 6) pays for that offset. The
contrast `pred(do v) - pred(do a_obs)` cancels it. The ordering is the same under both:
a hidden seasonal driver takes NaiveOLS from 0.819 and 0.769 on the plain structures to
0.617 and 0.595, a hidden trend to 0.722 and 0.683, and on the observed labels
BackDoorOLS, which adjusts for D, scores 0.719 to 0.766 against 0.585 to 0.720 for the
regression that ignores it. BackDoorOLS declines the plain `bi_variate` and the hidden
labels and then equals the Mean row.

## TimeOLS: modelling time recovers a hidden driver (October 2026)

A hidden driver is a sinusoid or a straight line in time, so the effect stays
identifiable by modelling time. `TimeOLS` (`dotime.baselines`) tests that. It is
NaiveOLS's regression `Y_t ~ 1 + A_t + Y_(t-1)` plus a linear trend and, when the
Bayesian information criterion prefers it, one seasonal harmonic whose period is
searched on the pre-onset rows. It predicts at the query's own time and adjusts
for nothing else. `time_ols.py` rebuilds the full suite from the release config
and scores it with the packaged protocol, `evaluate(dir_target="effect",
impute=True)`. Gate: Mean, NaiveOLS and BackDoorOLS reproduce
`seasonal_trend_per_label.json` exactly on every label, so the local episodes are
the released ones. The per-arm target statistics are asserted first.

the factual treatment at the intervened row, isolates the effect estimate (same
valid episodes; a zero contrast counts as wrong, so the declining BackDoorOLS

Findings:

1. On every hidden-driver label TimeOLS scores 0.78 to 0.83, back at the level of
   the plain structures (0.82 and 0.78), where NaiveOLS scores 0.60 to 0.72. Its
   own effect has the right sign in 0.86 to 0.93 of the episodes, against 0.57
   to 0.80 for NaiveOLS. Modelling time recovers the effect of a hidden driver,
   as the suite's documentation says it should.
2. Without a driver TimeOLS costs nothing: +0.003 and +0.014 on the plain
   structures, both intervals covering zero.
3. On observed-driver labels TimeOLS has the higher packaged score (0.79 to 0.84
   against 0.72 to 0.77 for BackDoorOLS), but BackDoorOLS, which adjusts for the
   released D itself, has the better effect estimate (own-effect sign 0.91 to
   0.94 against 0.86 to 0.93). TimeOLS's lead there comes from forecasting the
   driver's level at the query, which BackDoorOLS averages over the pre-onset
   rows (finding 6 above).
4. TimeOLS adjusts only for time, so on `back_door` labels the confounder X stays
   unadjusted. Its own-effect sign there is 0.86 to 0.89, against 0.88 to 0.93 on
   the `bi_variate` labels with the same driver.
